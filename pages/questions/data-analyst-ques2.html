10 Most-Asked Data Analyst Interview Questions (PART 2)

Prepare effectively for your next Data Analyst interview with these commonly asked questions and model answers.

1. What is the difference between Data Mining and Data Profiling?

Data Profiling focuses on analyzing individual datasets to understand their structure, quality, and content. Data Mining involves searching for hidden patterns, trends, or insights within large datasets to predict future outcomes.

2. What are the most common problems data analysts face during analysis?

Common hurdles include missing values, duplicate records, inconsistent data formatting (like different date formats), and "dirty" data that requires significant cleaning before it can be used for modeling.

3. Explain the difference between JOIN and UNION in SQL.

JOIN combines data from two or more tables based on a related column between them (horizontally). UNION combines the result-set of two or more SELECT statements into a single result (vertically), removing duplicates by default.

4. What is 'Data Cleansing' and why is it important?

Data cleansing is the process of identifying and correcting errors, inconsistencies, and inaccuracies in a dataset. It is vital because "garbage in, garbage out"—the quality of your insights depends entirely on the quality of your data.

5. What is an Outlier?

An outlier is a data point that differs significantly from other observations in a dataset. They can be caused by experimental error or indicate a rare, significant event that requires further investigation.

6. What is the difference between Descriptive and Predictive Analytics?

Descriptive analytics looks at historical data to understand "what happened" (e.g., monthly sales reports). Predictive analytics uses historical data and statistical modeling to forecast "what might happen" in the future.

7. Explain the importance of a Normal Distribution.

In a Normal Distribution, data is symmetrical around the mean (the "bell curve"). It is important because many statistical tests assume the data follows this distribution, and it allows analysts to calculate probabilities and identify outliers easily.

8. What is a Pivot Table?

A Pivot Table is a data summarization tool found in spreadsheet programs (like Excel) or data frames. It allows you to automatically sort, count, total, or average data stored in one large table, transforming rows into columns to see different perspectives.

9. What are the common steps in a data analytics project?

The standard workflow includes: 1. Defining the Problem, 2. Data Collection, 3. Data Cleaning, 4. Data Exploration (EDA), 5. Data Modeling/Analysis, and 6. Data Visualization/Reporting.

10. What is the difference between qualitative and quantitative data?

Quantitative data is numerical and can be measured or counted (e.g., price, weight). Qualitative data is descriptive and categorical, often based on traits or characteristics (e.g., color, names, feedback text).
